Suggested plan: an Open Repositories proposal by 17 January 2027; a
15-page TPDL paper by 9 April 2027; JCDL or iPRES if TPDL declines.

## Open Repositories 2027

- Theme: "Who Owns Knowledge? Issues of Sovereignty, Ownership, and Social
  Impact for Repositories", with the sub-themes Sovereignty, Community
  Ownership, Resilience ("How do we ensure our services stay up, open,
  available and able to recover from situations that can destroy critical
  information"), Attribution, Credit, Relevance, and Social Impact.
- Categories: presentations (20 minutes; 2-3 pages), workshops and
  tutorials, birds of a feather (at most 2 pages), repository carnival,
  24x7 lightning talks, posters (1 page), panels (2-3 pages). At most four
  submissions per author. The use of generative AI must be disclosed.
- Submissions open 31 October 2026, close 17 January 2027; notification 21
  February 2027. Presenters must register (from February 2027).
- Fit: the paper's case (authors own their record; archives rely on one
  another) matches the theme, and the repository and COAR community that
  adoption needs is there. A talk is not a publication, so it can go
  alongside a paper elsewhere.

## iPRES 2027

- Theme: "Authenticity, Accountability, and Adaptability". Authenticity asks
  how "digital preservation practices can contribute to trusted digital
  content"; accountability, how to keep an ecosystem "FAIR and TRUSTed"
  and data sovereignty.
- Deadlines: peer-reviewed 12 March 2027, non-peer-reviewed 14 April 2027.
  Categories, limits, template and proceedings: not yet published.
- Fit: the preservation side of the paper (fixity, pins, mirrors,
  takedown, tombstones, the rights of copies), and the OCFL and Software
  Heritage communities.

## TPDL 2027

- 31st conference. Two tracks with one programme and one volume: on the
  call page "Computer Science Foundations for Digital Libraries" and
  "Digital Humanities and Digital Libraries" (the news page calls them
  qualitative and quantitative).
- Limits: full papers 15 pages, short and vision papers 8, demonstrations
  6, doctoral consortium 4, each plus unlimited references; Springer style
  (Overleaf template available).
- Dates (AoE, tentative): workshops 20 February, abstracts 2 April, papers 9
  April, notification 30 May, camera-ready July 2027.
- Topics that fit: "Digital Preservation and Curation", "FAIR Data and
  Software", "Research Objects: FAIR Digital Objects (FDO), RO-Crate",
  "Federated Repositories and Data Spaces", "Data Citation and Credit
  Distribution", "Scholarly Communication Knowledge Graphs",
  "Nanopublications".
- Fit: the best for the paper as written, and no anonymisation is stated.
  The current text before the references would take about 18-20 LNCS pages,
  so it must be cut to 15 (mostly section 3, the format).

## JCDL 2027

No call yet (the 2027 site did not load on 23 November 2026). The 2026
call, as a guide:

- Full papers 10 pages, short 4, references not counted (in 2025 they were
  counted, and the template was IEEE's); ACM `sigconf`.
- Double-blind: no author names, affiliations, acknowledgements, "project
  URLs that reveal identity" or non-anonymised references to the authors'
  own work; anonymous.4open.science is suggested for code. "Anonymized works
  that are available as preprints (e.g., on arXiv or SSRN) may be submitted
  without citing them."
- 2026 dates: papers 15 July (extended from 30 June), notification 25
  August, camera-ready 20 September; conference 13-16 October, Dallas,
  hybrid. One registration per paper; one author presents live.
- Strong papers may be invited to an IJDL special issue, extended by at
  least 30% (for 2026: submissions until 31 July 2027).
- Fit: the closest community, but anonymising OpenSheaf (a public project,
  the author's archive, its URLs) would take work.

## Journals, for a longer or different version

- International Journal on Digital Libraries (Springer): a longer version.
- Learned Publishing, Insights: a shorter version on unbundling, for
  publishers and librarians.
- Code4Lib Journal: the tools, the Manager, building the first archive.
- Information Services and Use: the comparison with nanopublications.

## What reviewers will likely ask

- Evaluation beyond the formal model and one archive: verification time
  and size for the 83 documents, the cost of a mirror, steps for a deposit,
  a small study of key management.
- More of the digital-library literature (Memento, versioning of preprints,
  persistent identifiers).
